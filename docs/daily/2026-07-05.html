Delegated Human Credentials for AI Agents: World ID's Quota Architecture
As AI agents act autonomously on behalf of users, World ID is introducing delegated quota systems where a verified human can grant an agent a scoped share of their 'one-per-human' proof — preventing agents from circumventing per-human caps at scale. This is architecturally distinct from OAuth delegation: rather than delegating permissions to resources, it delegates a fraction of a uniqueness proof. The abuse surface here is novel and largely untested at scale.

SEC IAM & cybersecurity 1 item
Proof-of-Human Identity Layer: Iris-Based SMPC Uniqueness at Billion-Person Scale
World ID's architecture addresses a structural gap in IAM: existing systems (SSO, passkeys, OAuth) solve authentication (1-to-1 credential match) but not uniqueness (1-to-many deduplication across global population). At billion-person scale, per-comparison iris error rates must be ~1-in-100-billion; they achieve this via multispectral hardware (the Orb) combined with anonymized multi-party computation (AMPC) where no single party ever holds a reconstructible biometric. The nullifier pattern — scoped, unlinkable tokens per application — means a user's activity across relying parties cannot be correlated, a property absent from standard federated identity flows.

FIN Fintech & fraud 1 item
Bot-Resistant Checkout Infrastructure: Why Standard Fraud Controls Fail Inventory Attacks
IP rate limits, CAPTCHAs, phone verification, and device fingerprinting all fail because they bind verification to cheap-to-acquire proxies rather than to a unique person — residential proxy networks, bulk SIMs, and randomised fingerprints defeat each layer systematically. For fintech and e-commerce fraud teams, the implication is that bot-resistant gating on high-value scarce inventory requires a uniqueness primitive, not just stronger authentication. World ID's approach is one candidate; the unlinkability and recovery properties are the key differentiators from existing KYC flows.

SWE Distributed systems & SWE 1 item
Secret-Shared Biometric Recovery: Treating Verified Identity as Account-Style Key Rotation
Rather than anchoring a lifelong credential to a single device private key (catastrophic on device loss), World ID's recovery model treats the verified human as an abstract account — keys can be rotated while the underlying uniqueness proof persists, using secret sharing across independent custodians in separate legal jurisdictions. This is analogous to SSS-based wallet recovery but applied to biometric-derived identity roots. The AMPC cryptographic paper underpinning this is at https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/705 and is worth reviewing for teams building high-assurance non-hardware-bound identity.
